Hotel recommendation pipeline results

Memory-based CF, model-based CF, content-based and hybrid recommenders were trained on 1.22 million HotelRec (TripAdvisor) reviews, and users' aspect preferences and traveler personas were estimated. Booking.com (Kaggle) reviews were used to check whether this also works for new users on another platform. The numbers come from the CSVs in Pipeline/outputs/ (seed 42).

02 · Regression-based aspect importance and personas

Aspect importance was estimated with a linear regression explaining the overall rating by the 6 sub-ratings. Users have few reviews, so the per-user weights use a Ridge that shrinks toward the global weights.

Global aspect weights (R² = 0.761)

rooms and service influence the overall rating the most, location the least.

val RMSE by personalization strength λ

The dashed line is the global weights (0.4565). The larger λ (the weaker the personalization), the lower the error, so the per-user weights were no better than the global weights.

Persona centroids (K=3, silhouette 0.352)

The importance shares of the three personas differ by only about 0.003, so they can hardly be called different traveler types. That is why 04·05 re-estimate the preferences from deviations instead of regression coefficients.

04·05 · Deviation-based preferences and personas

Averages how much higher or lower the user's aspect scores are than that hotel's mean. How generously the user scores all aspects is leniency; after removing leniency, how harshly the user scores a particular aspect compared with others is strictness. Only means are taken, no coefficients are estimated, so it is stable even with few reviews.

test sub-rating prediction RMSE (all aspects)

Leniency gives the largest improvement (0.905 → 0.878). Per-aspect personal strictness (−0.001) and personas (−0.0002) have small effects. Settings were chosen on val and test is reported separately.

val RMSE by strictness shrinkage strength M

leniency + personal strictnessleniency only

With weak shrinkage (M=1, 3) noise makes it worse than leniency only; M=30 is best.

Per-aspect leniency coefficient β (val)

Only 0.46~0.67 times the leniency carries over to each aspect. location is smallest because the hotel itself matters more there than the user's tendency.

Number of personas K (val)

Increasing K changes val RMSE by less than 0.0004, so the simplest K=2 was chosen.

Selected personas (M=30, K=2)

Strictness is the score difference (points) from the hotel mean. + means the user rates that aspect more harshly than others. "Strict on service" is harsh overall (leniency −0.08), "Strict on sleep" is generous (+0.07). The per-user assignment is in data/pipeline/user_personas_dev_tuned.csv.

06 · Rating prediction

Error of predicting the ratings of all users' test reviews. Lower is better.

test RMSE

The dashed line is the baseline (μ+b_u+b_i). The improved ItemKNN is on par with the baseline; the original ItemKNN was worse than even the mean prediction.

MF learning curve

valtrain

val RMSE is lowest at epoch 15 (0.876), so the parameters from that point are used. It got worse for 3 consecutive epochs afterwards and training stopped early.

  • The rating hybrid (MF 0.8 + strictness content 0.2) is best (0.906), followed by MF alone (0.907) and the baseline (0.909). The gaps are small because users have 7 reviews on average, leaving little to gain from personalization.
  • Changing ItemKNN's baseline prediction from the user mean to μ+b_u+b_i took RMSE from 1.034 to 0.909. However, the user has rated a neighbor of the target hotel in only 23% of cases, so the rest fall back to the baseline prediction.

06·07 · Top-N recommendation

The correct hotel (the one the user actually went to next) is mixed with candidates, and the share ranked in the top 10 (HR@10) is measured. Both random candidates from around the world and candidates from the same city as the correct hotel are reported.

Random candidates (1 correct + 99) · 20,000 users

The dashed line is random ranking (0.10).

Same-city candidates (1 correct + 49) · 4,975 users

The dashed line is random ranking (0.20). Only users in cities with 50+ hotels are evaluated.

HR@10 by user history (train reviews), random candidates

ItemKNN improves with more history and beats popularity from 5 reviews on. Content-based is slightly better the less history there is.

val HR@10 by ItemKNN number of neighbors (k) (07)

Ranking uses the sum of co-visit similarities to the hotels the user visited. More neighbors raise the overlap with the user's history from 7% to 23% and improve the ranking. The original version (user-mean baseline, k=50) scored 0.079.

Hybrid weight λ selection (val)

The hybrid is split in two by purpose. Rating hybrid = λ·MF + (1−λ)·strictness content, λ chosen by val RMSE. Ranking hybrid = λ·ItemKNN + (1−λ)·strictness content (both scores standardized within the user's candidates, then summed), λ chosen by val NDCG@10. Both hybrids use λ=0.8, so CF and content actually mix. It was split this way because combining MF and content by NDCG chose λ=0 (content only), since both are weak at ranking.

06 · Plugging the deviation-based preferences into recommendation

The leniency, strictness and personas from 04·05 were put into the recommendation models of 06. The content-based aspect weights are tilted toward the aspects the user is strict about (weight ∝ global weight × exp(τ × strictness)), and κ × leniency is added to the predicted rating. A variant that uses the persona's strictness instead of the personal one for users with little history (fewer than 5 train reviews, 42.6%) was also compared.

val NDCG@10 by strictness strength τ

Highest at τ=160 (0.0729). There the single aspect the user is strictest about takes about 89% of the weight, close to "choosing hotels mainly on the strictest aspect". The leniency coefficient κ is 0.51.

HR@10 of users with 20+ reviews (random candidates)

The strictness effect is largest for users who wrote many reviews (0.110 → 0.119~0.123). For users with 3~4 reviews it is almost unchanged, 0.141 → 0.142.

Rating RMSE improved from 0.952 to 0.931, mostly thanks to the leniency correction, but is still higher than MF and Baseline (0.907~0.909). Ranking improves slightly on random candidates (HR@5 +11%) and not at all on same-city candidates. PersonaPopularity, regression or deviation, is similar to popularity.

08~10 · Kaggle text validation

Each Booking.com review was treated as one new user, and aspect keyword mentions were counted in the positive and negative reviews (466,835 reviews, mentions found in 90.6%).

Families mention location relatively more; business and solo travelers mention rooms, sleep_quality and value more. Travelers with friends is a small sample of 1,930 reviews.

Spearman correlation of hotel aspect reputation scores with reviewer scores

The global weights learned on HotelRec correlate better than equal weights for every trip type (0.274 → 0.301). The regression-based persona weights are almost the same as the global weights.

How much the result changes when one keyword is removed (09)

The largest change (pp) in the mention deviation by trip type. The two words location and clean account for about 79% of their aspects' mentions and drive the result the most.

Revised keyword lexicon (10)

  • 4 removed: bed·beds (rooms, overlaps with sleep), smell (cannot tell good from bad smells), cheap (used for quality complaints rather than price)
  • 21 added: waiter·manager·housekeeping etc. to service, overcharged·reasonable etc. to value, thin walls·uncomfortable·snoring etc. to sleep_quality
  • Conclusions hold: with the revised lexicon the most-mentioned aspect per trip type is the same in 7 of 8 groups and the least-mentioned in all 8. The one change is Solo traveler (sleep_quality → rooms).

The list is in outputs/10_lexicon_changes.csv, the robustness in 10_lexicon_robustness.csv.

Kaggle validation by persona set (10, assigned by all review text)

The association between traveler type and assigned persona (Cramér's V) is weak for every combination, 0.03~0.05. The deviation-based (04, M=3) persona weights correlate best with reviewer scores (0.312), but the gap to the global weights (0.301) is small.

How the methods were improved

What problems showed up with the first approaches, and how they were changed to reach the current values. All settings were chosen on val; test was reported separately. Detailed tables are in "7. How the methods were improved" of Pipeline/README.md.

① Data mapping (preprocessing)

  1. Firstused hotel_map.csv (162,023 rows) from the team Drive → city name appears in that hotel's reviews 3.9% of the time
  2. Causea mapping from a different run before the sampling step. user_map.csv was also missing, so no validation was possible
  3. Fixreran the preprocessing on the raw data → train/partial byte-identical to the shared files, only the 2 mappings replaced
  4. Nowcity name match 87.9%, validation 24/24 PASS. The same-city evaluation and Kaggle matching became possible

② Top-N evaluation

  1. First1 correct + 99 random hotels from around the world → popularity HR@10 0.328, more than twice the personalized models (MF 0.117)
  2. Causerandom candidates are mostly unpopular hotels in other regions, told apart by popularity alone
  3. Fixafter fixing the mapping, added the same-city candidates (1+49) evaluation
  4. Nowboth reported. In the same city: popularity 0.479, ItemKNN 0.392

③ Memory-based CF: Item-kNN

  1. Firstadjusted cosine (as in the proposal): val RMSE 1.012, worse than the mean prediction (0.974), HR@10 0.049
  2. Fix 1baseline prediction μ+b_u+b_i (KNNBaseline): RMSE 0.877
  3. Fix 2co-visit score for ranking: HR@10 0.216
  4. Fix 3neighbors 50 → 2000 (neighbor overlap 7% → 23%): HR@10 0.371
  5. Nowtest RMSE 1.034 → 0.909, HR@10 0.035 → 0.349 (beats popularity 0.328)

④ User aspect preferences and personas

  1. Firstregression-based personal weights (02): the stronger the personalization, the larger the val error; the 3 personas differ by about 0.003
  2. Causeusers have 3.7 reviews with complete sub-ratings on average, and the aspects are highly correlated
  3. Fix 1leniency and strictness from the deviation from the hotel mean (04): test sub-rating RMSE 0.905 → 0.890
  4. Fix 2shrinkage strength M=3 → 30, per-aspect leniency β (05): 0.877, "Strict on service" and "Strict on sleep" personas
  5. Fix 3plugged into recommendation, τ range widened and τ=160 chosen (06)
  6. Nowcontent-based RMSE 0.952 → 0.931, random HR@5 0.070 → 0.078. No difference in the same city

⑤ Hybrid

  1. Firstλ·MF + (1−λ)·content, λ chosen by val NDCG → λ=0, same as content-based
  2. CauseMF and content are both weak at ranking, while val RMSE with 90% MF was lower than MF alone
  3. Fixsplit by purpose: rating = MF + content (λ by RMSE), ranking = ItemKNN + content (per-user standardization, λ by NDCG)
  4. Nowboth hybrids at λ=0.8. Rating RMSE 0.906 (best), ranking on par with ItemKNN (NDCG@10 0.211)

⑥ Kaggle generalization check

  1. Firstaspect mention analysis with a keyword lexicon (08): clear differences by trip type, weak persona association (V 0.034)
  2. Auditkeyword sensitivity (09): location and clean are about 79% of the mentions; removing one changes the result by up to 4.7pp
  3. Fixre-compared with the revised lexicon (4 removed, 21 added) (10): trip-type conclusions hold, persona association still weak
  4. Nowmatched 505 shared hotels (11): HotelRec hotel scores correlate 0.74 with Booking.com hotel means

Limitations and next steps

  • Effect of the hybrids: the rating hybrid improves by about 0.001, and the ranking hybrid does not differ from ItemKNN alone on test (content-based adds almost nothing to ranking). Popularity is still strongest on same-city candidates.
  • Use of personas: the deviation-based preferences are plugged into 06's recommendation, but the improvements are small. They are not plugged into the Kaggle new-user prediction (11) because there is no user history.
  • Evaluation coverage: the same-city evaluation covers only users in large cities (25% of the sample), and the Kaggle matching only 505 hotels in 6 European cities.
  • Hand-made rules: the aspect keyword lexicon (08, revised in 10) and the hotel name matching rules (11) were set by the team. The match list can be checked in outputs/11_matched_hotels.csv.
  • Deviation baseline: the hotel means in 04 include the user's own train reviews, so for hotels with few reviews the deviation comes out slightly smaller.
